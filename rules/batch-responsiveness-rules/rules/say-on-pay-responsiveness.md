# Say-on-Pay Responsiveness

The rule text the two runs in this folder read (wording of 16 November 2026).

Vote AGAINST the say-on-pay item where support for say-on-pay at the company's previous annual meeting was below 70%, unless this proxy shows both (a) and (b); otherwise vote FOR under this rule. Support is votes for divided by votes for plus against, from the company's report of the vote, counting a controlling holder like any other; abstentions, broker non-votes and the company's own percentage or result do not count. Read the response from this proxy: what it does not show, the company has not done.

(a) Engagement. The company sought holders' views after the vote. Judge this as an experienced analyst would, from the whole disclosure. Weigh when the company met holders, how often, how many it reached and whether an independent director took part; none is required on its own. A general statement such as "we value shareholder feedback" does not show it, and neither does outreach the proxy does not say took place after the vote.

(b) Response. The board acted, since the vote, on what holders raised: a change to pay design, a pay decision (such as a smaller award), or a commitment not to repeat a named practice. Judge this as an experienced analyst would; naming holders' concerns makes the link easy to see but is not required on its own. A commitment counts even if it keeps an exception for extraordinary circumstances, as long as the practice has not been repeated since the vote. More disclosure alone is not an action. If holders gave no specific feedback, a reason for the low support and one such action are enough.

Below 50% support, only a change to pay design or a pay decision counts under (b).

The rationale names last year's support and what is missing and, where present, low support in earlier years, a controlling holder, a pending merger or spin-off, and an unimplemented majority-supported pay proposal. These do not change the vote. With no say-on-pay item on this ballot, or none at the previous annual meeting, this rule does not apply; the Compensation Committee Accountability rule covers the committee.
