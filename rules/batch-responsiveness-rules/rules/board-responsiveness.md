# Board Responsiveness to Shareholder Votes

The rule text the two runs in this folder read (wording of 16 November 2026).

Vote AGAINST or WITHHOLD from the members of the nominating/governance committee (or the committee that nominates directors) standing for election where the board failed to act on any of the following. Membership is as of the meeting date: the proxy's roster plus any change a filing says takes effect by then. With no such committee, or none of its members standing, vote against every director standing who was on the board at the previous annual meeting.

(a) a shareholder proposal, other than one on executive pay, with more than 50% support at the company's previous annual meeting;
(b) a management proposal to ratify an existing charter or bylaw provision with less than 50% support at that meeting;
(c) a director with less than 50% support at that meeting; or
(d) a tender offer, made since that meeting, into which more than half the outstanding shares were tendered.

Support is votes for divided by votes for plus against or withheld, from the company's report of the vote, counting a controlling holder like any other. Abstentions, broker non-votes and the company's own percentage or result do not count.

The board acted on (a) if it implemented the proposal in full, on the terms it asked for, and on (b) if it removed or amended the provision. It also acted on either if a binding management proposal on this ballot does so, with the board recommending FOR. Otherwise it acted on (a) or (b) only if the proxy, or a filing between the vote and the proxy, shows a real response to the vote. Judge this as an experienced analyst would: weigh contact with holders about the vote after it, the reason given for not acting in full, and any step taken, such as a proposal that implements less; none is required on its own. Saying the current terms serve holders well, or promising to keep the matter under review, is not a response. Neither is a reason for not acting, with no contact with holders after the vote and no step taken.

The board acted on (c) if the director has left or the company has announced the director will leave or not be renominated, or if the proxy names the concern behind the vote and a change since that removes it. A change removes the concern only if what holders objected to no longer exists, such as a board seat given up or a practice ended; more disclosure is not. If not, also vote against that director if standing.

The board acted on (d) if it removed every defense blocking the offer, agreed to a sale, or put the offer to a shareholder vote.

Only the proxy and filings between the vote and the proxy count: a tender offer or a response that none of them discloses has not been made. If the board acted on every item, vote FOR under this rule. With no previous annual meeting, this rule does not apply. A shareholder proposal on executive pay falls under the Compensation Committee Accountability rule. The rationale names the proposal or director, last year's support, any controlling holder, and what part of a response is missing.
